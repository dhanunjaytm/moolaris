02 / PRODUCT-DEVELOPMENT WORKFLOWAN INTENDED, ITERATIVE APPROACH

Every stage asks
what should happen next?

Select a stage to see what may be explored and what evidence can guide the next decision. Not every project will proceed through every stage.

  1. 01Problem＋

    Clarify who experiences the need, where it occurs, and why current options may fall short.

    Next decision: Is the need specific and worth investigating?
  2. 02Research＋

    Study existing products, users, workflows, constraints, and relevant technical approaches.

    Next decision: What is already solved, and what remains unclear?
  3. 03Validate＋

    Check assumptions with available evidence, stakeholder input, and practical context.

    Next decision: Is there enough evidence to shape a product opportunity?
  4. 04Concept＋

    Compare possible approaches and outline a concept against clear requirements.

    Next decision: Which concept best addresses the validated need?
  5. 05Engineer＋

    Develop the system architecture, electronics, firmware, software, or integration plan needed for the concept.

    Next decision: Is the design practical to build and test within the agreed scope?
  6. 06Prototype＋

    Where capability and scope allow, assemble a prototype to investigate behavior and design assumptions.

    Next decision: What does the prototype demonstrate, and what needs refinement?
  7. 07Demonstrate＋

    Show the prototype or technical evidence in a relevant use context and record what is learned.

    Next decision: What feedback or test evidence should shape the next iteration?
  8. 08Market＋

    Explore user response, adoption barriers, alternatives, and potential routes to market.

    Next decision: Is there a clear user and viable path worth pursuing?
  9. 09Revenue＋

    If the evidence supports it, examine possible pricing, costs, and sustainable business models.

    Next decision: Does a workable commercial model appear possible?
  10. 10Scale＋

    Consider production, support, and growth only when product performance, demand, and delivery requirements are better understood.

    Next decision: What capabilities and investment might future growth require?

✳ Revenue, manufacturing, investment, and scale are possible future stages—not secured outcomes or commitments.
